I am a student of mathematical physics and more broadly of science as a discipline of mind. Throughout my studies, I've been particularly drawn to the guiding role of symmetry and the richness of the mathematical structures underlying quantum field theories. I have greatly enjoyed applying the tools of representation theory and algebraic geometry to the study of strongly coupled supersymmetric gauge theories. I am also drawn to the potential of modern computational tools, particularly machine learning. I believe that mathematical reasoning and learning systems have much to offer one another, and that cultivating this relationship will be increasingly important in directing their capabilities toward a better understanding of reality.

Research

5D Supersymmetric Gauge Theories at Infinite Coupling: Brane Webs, Quivers, and Chiral Rings

PDF

MSc dissertation · Imperial College London · Supervisor: Prof. Amihay Hanany

September 2026

Investigated the role of the Chern–Simons level in the hyperkähler Higgs-branch moduli spaces of 5d gauge-matter theories using Type IIB (p,q)-brane webs and 3d Coulomb branch quiver techniques. Identified physical generators and derived explicit polynomial relation ideals for the coordinate rings of six SU(3) theories at infinite coupling, establishing the instanton operator completions of perturbative multiplets under enhanced global symmetries and extending existing Hilbert series and highest-weight generating function results through plethystic methods. Proved finite-coupling glueball nilpotency across all six theories, demonstrating its independence from the Chern–Simons level, and conjectured a link between successive glueball powers, mesonic rank stratification and nilpotent-orbit closures, with evidence from a higher-rank SU(4) theory.

Quantum Machine Learning

GitHub

Summer 2025

EPSRC-funded research · QET Labs, University of Bristol · With Dr Giulia Rubino, Dr Marius Krumm and Shivani Datye

Developed a differentiable JAX simulator of a custom multimode photonics architecture, combining parameterised interferometers, data re-uploading and permanent-based measurement statistics. Investigated circuit parameterisations, data encoding strategies and input Fock states to improve classification performance under realistic photon loss. Scaled training to the BluePebble HPC cluster using SLURM and developed a customisable experimentation framework incorporating parameter sweeps, checkpointing and logging to streamline training runs and support future PhD projects.

Lattice spin systems

PDF

Spring 2025

Extended lab project · University of Bristol · Supervisor: Prof. Francesco Turci

Compared Metropolis–Hastings and Wolff cluster Monte Carlo algorithms in the study of critical phenomena and critical slowing down in the two-dimensional Ising model, alongside further investigations of the XY and Heisenberg models. Applied finite-size scaling and Binder cumulants to calculate critical exponents, benchmarking simulations against Onsager’s exact solution.
